# Winter prep guide

## Before the first freeze
Disconnect garden hoses and drain them. Shut off and drain outdoor faucets if your house has an inside shutoff valve. Clean gutters so melting snow can drain instead of forming ice dams. Check weatherstripping on doors; if you can see daylight around a closed door, replace it or add a draft stopper.

## Windows
Window insulation kits use a clear plastic film and a hair dryer to seal drafty windows for the season. One kit covers up to five standard windows. The film comes off cleanly in spring.

## Heating safety
Test smoke detectors and carbon monoxide detectors monthly and replace batteries once a year. Keep space heaters three feet away from anything that can burn, plug them directly into a wall outlet (never into an extension cord or power strip), and turn them off when you leave the room.

## Ice melt or rock salt?
**Rock salt** is the cheapest option but stops working below about **15°F**, and it can damage concrete less than a year old and harm plants. **Our ice melt** is a calcium chloride blend that works down to **-15°F** and is gentler on concrete. A 50 lb bag of ice melt treats roughly **1,000 square feet** per application. Neither product is safe for pets to eat; wipe paws after walks.

## Shoveling
Push snow instead of lifting it when you can. A 24 inch shovel moves more snow per pass; an 18 inch shovel is easier for heavy, wet snow and for people who want a lighter load. Shovel early and often rather than waiting for the storm to end.

## Pipes
Pipes along outside walls are the most likely to freeze. On very cold nights, open cabinet doors under sinks to let warm air in and let a faucet drip slightly. Foam pipe insulation is cheap and easy to install on exposed pipes in basements and crawl spaces.
