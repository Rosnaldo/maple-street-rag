# Paint counter guide

## Choosing a sheen
Flat paint hides wall flaws best but marks easily, so it suits ceilings and low-traffic rooms. Eggshell has a soft glow and wipes clean, which makes it the most popular choice for living rooms and bedrooms. Satin is a step shinier and stands up to kitchens, hallways and kids' rooms. Semi-gloss is for trim, doors and bathrooms, where moisture and scrubbing are common. Gloss is rarely used on walls; it shows every bump.

## Interior or exterior
Interior paint is made to resist scuffs and to be low-odor. Exterior paint is made to flex with temperature changes and to resist fading and mildew. Do not use interior paint outside; it will peel within a season. Exterior paint can be used inside a garage or workshop but has a stronger odor while it cures.

## Preparing the surface
Wash walls with mild soap and water and let them dry. Fill holes with spackle, let it dry, and sand it smooth. Scrape any loose or peeling paint. Glossy surfaces need a light sanding so new paint can grip. Tape trim with painter's tape and press the edge down firmly. Remove switch plates and outlet covers rather than painting around them.

## When you need primer
Use primer on new drywall, on bare wood, on patched areas larger than a hand, on stains (water, smoke, marker), and when going from a very dark color to a light one. Drywall primer is sold by the gallon. Primer is not usually needed when repainting a wall in good condition in a similar color.

## Weather and drying
For exterior work, paint when the temperature is between 50 and 90 degrees Fahrenheit and no rain is expected for 24 hours. Interior latex paint is dry to the touch in about one hour and ready for a second coat in about four hours. It reaches full hardness after about two weeks, so wait before scrubbing.

## Tools
A 9 inch roller with a 3/8 inch nap is right for most smooth interior walls. Use a 1/2 inch nap for lightly textured walls and 3/4 inch for heavily textured surfaces or stucco. An angled 2 inch brush is best for cutting in along ceilings and trim.

## How much paint to buy
One gallon of our interior latex paint covers **350 to 400 square feet per coat** on a smooth, primed wall. On textured walls, plan on about **250 square feet per gallon per coat**. We recommend **two coats** for an even finish, especially for a color change. To estimate: multiply the wall area by the number of coats, divide by the coverage, and round up to a whole gallon. A quart covers about one quarter of what a gallon covers and is good for touch-ups and small accent areas.

To measure a wall, multiply its width by its height. Subtract about 20 square feet for each standard door and about 15 square feet for each window.

## Color matching
We can match a color from a physical sample. The sample must be **at least 1 inch square**, flat, and clean. Fabric, paper and painted chips all work. We cannot match from a photo on a phone because screen colors are not accurate.

## Mixing time
Tinting and shaking a gallon takes 10 to 15 minutes. During busy weekend hours, please allow up to 30 minutes. The paint counter stops taking new mixing jobs 30 minutes before the store closes.

## Returns on paint
Untinted, unopened paint follows our normal return policy. Tinted or custom-mixed paint cannot be returned, so check your color sample on the wall with a quart first if you are unsure.

## Disposal
Never pour paint down a drain. Let small amounts of latex paint dry out in the can with the lid off, then throw the dry can away with household trash. For larger amounts, ask the paint counter about the town's household hazardous waste days.
